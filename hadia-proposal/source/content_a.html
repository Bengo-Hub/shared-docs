Executive summary

Hadia is a gift registry for the way Kenyan families already give. A couple, a parent to be or a graduate builds a list of things they would love, shares one link on WhatsApp, and guests either reserve a physical gift so nobody buys the same blender twice, or send money toward a gift or a general fund straight from M-Pesa. Hadia earns a small percentage of cash gifts and an optional one-time premium fee per event.

Codevertex proposes to build Hadia on the Codevertex platform that already runs identity, payments, accounting, escrow and messaging for businesses across our ecosystem. Only the parts that are unique to Hadia are new: the registry itself, the guest experience and the owner dashboard. Everything that touches money reuses services that are already in production and already handle M-Pesa collections, held funds, payouts, double entry books and audit trails.

The proposal in five lines

  • Twelve weeks from signing to a live product with pilot events, including a full week of user acceptance testing.
  • USD 4,750 fixed build price, inside the USD 1,500 to 5,000 range in Hadia's brief, paid in four milestones.
  • Running costs of about USD 100 to 200 a month, in line with the brief, covered by roughly 100 active events.
  • Gift money is held in Hadia's own account with a licensed payment provider (PayHero) and paid out automatically by rules Hadia sets. Codevertex never holds client funds.
  • Compliance is designed in from day one: Kenya's Data Protection Act, consumer protection, payments and anti-money laundering rules, accessibility and honest marketing.

The reason a product of this quality fits the budget is reuse. Building phone sign-in, M-Pesa collection, wallet accounting, escrow, approvals, automated payouts, receipts and messaging from scratch would cost several times the brief's upper figure and would take longer than three months. On our platform those pieces exist, are tested with real money, and only need to be configured and connected for Hadia.

What Hadia asked for

We reviewed the two documents Hadia shared: the Hadia landing page mock-up and the build report prepared for Tracey. The requirements below come straight from them.

The product, as described

  • Phone number and OTP sign-in, because it is more natural than email for this market.
  • A wishlist tied to an event with a name, date and type: wedding, baby shower, new home, graduation and birthday.
  • Items added by hand (title, description, price estimate, photo) or by pasting a link from any shop, with auto-fill where possible. Manual entry has to be a first-class path because most local retailers publish no clean product data.
  • A public link that looks good when pasted into WhatsApp.
  • Guests browse, reserve an item so it shows as taken for everyone, or contribute any amount toward an item or a general fund through an M-Pesa STK push.
  • An owner dashboard showing reservations and contributions, with payouts to the owner's own M-Pesa, less the platform fee.

How Hadia makes money

StreamBriefHow this design delivers it
Cash gifting feeAbout 3 to 5% of each contribution, deducted before payoutA commission rule in Hadia's books (default 4%, editable, with optional minimum and cap) applied automatically at every payout. Guests never pay extra.
Premium per eventAbout USD 2 to 3: custom link, printable list, faster or instant payoutA one-time in-app purchase (suggested KSh 300, set by Hadia) that unlocks a custom link, a branded PDF of the list, and instant payouts in place of the standard schedule.

What the brief warns about, and our answer

Concern in the briefOur answer
Holding other people's money brings regulation and trust requirements. Consider a licensed aggregator rather than going direct to Safaricom.Money is collected and held by PayHero, a payment aggregator, in Hadia's own account. Our treasury service keeps a separate ledger per registry, reconciles hourly, and pays out by rules. See chapters 9 and 15.
Daraja approval and business registration take time.A business and legal track runs in parallel from week one (chapter 17). PayHero onboarding replaces a direct Daraja application, which removes the slowest dependency.
Link auto-fill will be unreliable on local sites.Auto-fill is a helper only. The manual form is the main path and works on a slow phone (chapter 10).
Early payment bugs would damage word of mouth.The payment core is not new code. It is our production escrow module, with idempotent contributions, one release in flight per pot, automatic return of failed payouts and hourly reconciliation.
Keep money logic separate from everything else.It is a separate service. hadia-api never moves money; it asks treasury-api to, and listens for the result.

Benchmark: MyRegistry and Zola

Hadia named MyRegistry and Zola as references. Both are mature US products. We studied their public feature sets and help pages to decide what Hadia should copy, adapt for Kenya, or leave out.

CapabilityMyRegistryZolaHadia decision
Add items from any shopYes, universal registry, browser button and importYes, alongside its own storeAdopt. Paste a link or enter by hand. No browser extension in the MVP.
Cash fundsYes, advertised with 0% fund feesYes, card fee of about 2.5% paid by guest or couple, or a fee-free option through a third-party appAdapt. M-Pesa first. Guests pay exactly the gift amount. Hadia's fee is taken at payout and shown clearly up front.
Group gifting on one itemYes, on any itemYes, on any gift or fundAdopt. Any item can be reserved, funded in parts, or both.
Duplicate preventionCross-store purchase markingAutomatic for Zola store itemsAdopt. Atomic reservation; the item shows as taken instantly for everyone.
Gift tracker and thank-you listYesYes, gift tracker and thank-you managerAdopt. Gift log with guest messages, thank-you status and one-tap WhatsApp thank-you.
Wedding website and planning toolsChecklistsFull planning suiteLater. A simple event page with story and date is in scope. A full planning suite is not.
Own store, shipping, completion discountsRetailer partnershipsOwn store and shippingLeave out. Retailer programmes are largely unavailable in Kenya, as the brief notes. A partner referral field is ready for later.
Payout timingDepends on fund providerScheduled transfersAdapt. Automatic payouts by threshold and event date; instant payouts for premium events.

Sources consulted on 6 October 2026: MyRegistry guides on universal registries and its Wikipedia entry; Zola's registry page and help articles on group gifting and cash fund fees. Competitor features change often and are listed only to guide product decisions.

Two lessons shape Hadia's positioning. First, fee transparency is a selling point: both benchmarks talk about fees prominently, so Hadia will state its fee in plain words on the guest page and in the owner's settings. Second, the strongest registries make the guest's job effortless. For Hadia that means no account, no app download, and an M-Pesa prompt within two taps of opening the link.

Out of scope (can be quoted separately)

  • Native iOS and Android store apps. The PWA installs to the home screen and covers the MVP.
  • A full wedding planning suite, guest RSVP management and seating plans.
  • Physical gift delivery, shipping or a Hadia shop.
  • Retailer affiliate integrations and price tracking.
  • Formal legal opinions. We prepare drafts and a compliance map; Hadia's Kenyan advocate signs them off.
  • Paid advertising, video production and influencer campaigns from the go-to-market plan.
