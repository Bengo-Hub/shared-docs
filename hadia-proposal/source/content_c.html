Critical workflows, step by step

Each workflow lists the trigger, the steps, what is automated and what happens when something goes wrong. Steps marked auto need no person. The low-fidelity screens below show the three moments that matter most; final visuals follow Hadia's mock-up.

W1. Hadia business onboarding (once)

  1. Hadia provides company documents (certificate of incorporation, CR12, KRA PIN, director ID, bank or paybill details).
  2. Codevertex creates the Hadia tenant in auth-api and the plan with the escrow and M-Pesa features in subscriptions-api.
  3. Hadia's PayHero Team is created or linked to the tenant. Hadia completes PayHero KYC to tier 3 (national ID plus company KRA PIN), which PayHero requires before a Team wallet can collect from the public.
  4. Hadia's paybill or till is added as a channel and synced; the Team wallet and channels are mapped to accounts in Hadia's chart of accounts.
  5. Hadia accepts the escrow terms in treasury (Hadia holds its customers' money for their beneficiaries and is responsible for releasing it).
  6. Hadia sets the commission rule, payout policy and approval limit, contribution limits and sweep schedule.
  7. Live KSh 1 tests: a contribution, a payout, a refund and a sweep, each reconciled, before the first real event.

W2. Registry owner onboarding

  1. The owner enters a phone number. auth-api sends a 6-digit code by SMS (WhatsApp if SMS fails) auto. Codes are hashed, expire in 5 minutes, and sending is capped per phone and per IP.
  2. The owner confirms age 18 or above and accepts the terms and privacy notice. auth-api stores the version and time.
  3. The owner creates the event: type, title, date, optional place, cover photo, short story.
  4. The owner confirms the payout M-Pesa number with an OTP sent to it. This proves control of the line that will receive money.
  5. hadia-api asks treasury to open the escrow pot with the owner as beneficiary and Hadia's default rules auto.
  6. The registry gets its short link and preview image, and the owner shares it on WhatsApp.

When it goes wrong: if the pot cannot be opened (for example PayHero is unavailable), the registry is saved as a draft and the pot request is retried by a background job; the owner can keep adding items while it waits.

W3. Listing items

  1. The owner pastes a link or taps "Add by hand".
  2. For a link, hadia-api fetches the page server-side with strict limits: public internet addresses only (private and internal ranges refused, the same rule treasury uses for webhooks), 3 second timeout, 1 MB size cap, no cookies. It reads Open Graph and product schema tags for title, image and price.
  3. The form is prefilled and the owner confirms or edits. If nothing useful was found, the form simply stays empty for typing.
  4. Photos are resized and converted to WebP (640 px and 1280 px versions), stored in object storage, and served through the CDN. The source shop is shown as "from Shop name" with a link.
  5. The owner chooses the mode: reserve, fund, or both (group gifting).

W4. Sharing

  1. Each registry has a short link, for example hadia.co.ke/r/achieng-david (premium) or hadia.co.ke/r/K7Q2M9 (standard).
  2. A preview image is rendered per registry (cover photo, names, date) at 1200 by 630 pixels and kept under 300 KB, so WhatsApp shows it reliably auto.
  3. The share sheet offers WhatsApp, SMS, copy link and a QR code for printed cards.

W5. Reserving an item

  1. The guest taps Reserve, enters a name and, optionally, a phone number to manage the reservation.
  2. hadia-api reserves with one conditional update (only if units remain). If two guests tap at the same moment, exactly one wins; the other is told the item was just taken and offered to contribute instead.
  3. The guest receives a link to cancel. The owner sees the reservation, and other guests see the item as taken.
  4. After 14 days without "bought", the guest and owner are reminded; the owner may release the item auto.

W6. Contributing money

  1. The guest picks an item or fund, enters an amount and phone number. Limits are checked before anything is sent.
  2. hadia-ui calls treasury's public escrow contribution endpoint with an idempotency key, so a double tap or a retry can never charge twice.
  3. Treasury starts a PayHero collection into Hadia's wallet; the guest gets the M-Pesa prompt and enters the PIN.
  4. PayHero calls back. Treasury looks the payment up with PayHero before trusting it, then credits the pot once and posts the accounting entry auto.
  5. The checkout page, which polls every 3 seconds, shows success. Treasury publishes escrow.pot_credited.
  6. hadia-api updates the item's progress and the gift log, and asks notifications-api to send the guest a receipt and the owner an alert auto.

When it goes wrong: a prompt that fails or is cancelled leaves the gift pending and offers the paybill route. Treasury's prompt guard stops repeated prompts to one phone (5 failures in 24 hours) to protect guests from spam prompts. A callback that never arrives is resolved by a status lookup.

W7. Managing the pot

  1. The owner sees received, paid out, pending and next payout on the dashboard; numbers come from treasury and the gift log.
  2. The owner can change the target, the threshold (within Hadia's limits) and the event date. The payout number can be changed only with a fresh OTP on the new number and never while a payout is in flight.
  3. Items funded beyond their price show as fully funded; extra money stays in the pot and is paid out with the rest.
  4. After the event, the registry stays open for late gifts for the hold window, then closes to new gifts. The owner can reopen it.

W8. Automatic payouts

Described in full in chapter 9 and Figure 4. In short: every 5 minutes, pots whose threshold is reached or whose event hold has passed are paid out to the verified number, less commission, with approval only above Hadia's limit auto. The owner receives an SMS with the gross, the fee and the net.

W9. Commission and sweep

Described in chapter 9 and Figure 5. Commission is deducted at each payout, booked as Hadia's income, and swept daily to Hadia's own account above the floor auto. An eTIMS-compliant invoice for Hadia's fee is available to the owner on request through treasury's existing eTIMS integration.

W10. Premium upgrade

  1. The owner taps Upgrade on an event and sees exactly what premium adds and the price.
  2. hadia-api creates a payment intent in treasury (an ordinary sale for Hadia, not escrow) and opens the shared payment modal.
  3. On confirmation treasury books the sale as Hadia's revenue and publishes payment.succeeded; hadia-api switches on the features: custom link, printable list, instant payouts (pot threshold set to pay every gift) auto.
  4. A receipt is sent. Refunds of premium follow the refund policy.

W11. Cancellation and refunds

  1. The owner asks to cancel (for example, the event is called off), or Hadia cancels after a fraud or policy finding.
  2. The refund passes the approval policy, bound to the pot and its balance.
  3. The pot is closed first so no new gifts arrive, then each gift is paid back to the phone it came from, each with its own reference auto.
  4. A failed refund returns the money to the pot for retry. A repeated cancel never pays anyone twice. Gifts with no phone on record are listed for Hadia to settle by hand.

W12. Exceptions and reconciliation

  1. Hourly reconciliation runs per tenant auto.
  2. Exceptions go to one queue in the Hadia admin: failed payouts after retry, M-Pesa name mismatches, refunds needing manual action, reconciliation shortfalls, reported registries, approval requests.
  3. Each item shows the facts, the suggested action and who handled it. Finance gets a daily summary by email or WhatsApp.

W13. Thank-you tracker

  1. Every gift and reservation appears in the gift log with the guest's message.
  2. The owner taps Thank to open WhatsApp with a prepared message to that guest, then marks it thanked.
  3. A gentle reminder lists unthanked guests a week after the event (owner can switch it off).

W14. Data requests and account deletion

  1. An owner or guest asks for a copy of their data, a correction or deletion, from the privacy page or by email.
  2. Identity is confirmed by OTP. The request is logged with its legal deadline.
  3. Export: a machine-readable file of profile, registries, items, reservations and gifts.
  4. Deletion: personal data is erased or anonymised. Financial records required by tax law (contributions, payouts, invoices) are kept for the statutory period with the person's name replaced by a reference, and the person is told what was kept and why.

Data design

hadia-api owns the registry domain. It holds references to other services' records by ID only: the owner's user ID from auth-api and the pot ID and payment intent IDs from treasury.

Choices that keep it fast as data grows

  • Every table has the tenant ID and the indexes its queries need, created by versioned Atlas migrations reviewed like code.
  • Lists use keyset pagination (the shared pagination package), never deep offsets, so page 500 is as fast as page 1.
  • The gift log is unique on the payment intent ID, so a redelivered event cannot create a second row.
  • Counters such as an item's funded amount are updated with arithmetic in SQL in the same statement, never read then written.
  • Reports read from registry_daily_stats, a rollup updated as events arrive, not from raw gifts.
  • Guest phone numbers are stored hashed where only matching is needed (reservations) and encrypted where they must be read (receipts), with keys outside the database.
  • Photos live in object storage; the database stores keys only.
