# Staying safe

Scammers sometimes copy the look of a trusted service to get money or codes. These checks help hosts
and guests tell a real Hadia message from a fake one.

## Check the address

Every Hadia page and link uses **hadiafrica.com**. Before you pay, look at the address in your
browser. If it ends in anything else, even something that looks close, such as `hadia-africa.com` or
`hadiafrica.co`, it is not Hadia. Close the page and do not pay.

## Check the registry code

Each registry has its own code, and the host shares it with you. To be sure a link is real, open
`hadiafrica.com` yourself, press **Find a registry** and type the code. If the same registry opens,
the link is genuine.

## How you pay on Hadia

- You pay from the registry's own page on hadiafrica.com.
- An M-Pesa prompt comes to your phone. Enter your PIN only on that prompt.
- If the prompt does not reach you, the page shows paybill **756756** and an account number to use
  instead. That is the only paybill Hadia uses.

## What Hadia never asks for

Hadia never asks you, in a message, on a call or on any web page, for:

- your M-Pesa PIN;
- a code we sent you. Never read one out to anyone, even someone who says they work for Hadia;
- money sent to a personal phone number, or to any paybill other than 756756 with the account number
  shown on the registry page;
- money back. If anyone calls or messages asking you to return a payment, it is a scam.

Hosts: Hadia staff never ask for your PIN or a code, and never ask you to pay anything to release a
payout.

## If something looks wrong

1. Do not pay, and do not share any code.
2. Tell us at `hadiafrica.com/help`. Press **Contact us**, choose what it is about, and include the
   link or a screenshot of the message. You get a ticket number to follow the reply.
3. If it is about a registry, you can also press **Report this registry** at the foot of its page.
   The hosts are not told who sent the report.
4. Tell the host too, so they can warn their guests.
