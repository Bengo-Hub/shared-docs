Executive summary

Codevertex Property is a multi-tenant software-as-a-service platform for the Kenyan real estate market, built on the same shared services that already run Codevertex's commerce, finance and HR products. One platform serves three kinds of business, each as an isolated tenant with its own users, properties, money and brand:

  • Estate operators and developers such as Shaba Village, who sell homes and apartments outright or by instalments and then manage the estate and bill owners for water, service charge and shared services.
  • Property management firms that let and manage buildings for many landlords: they list units, onboard tenants on signed leases, collect rent and utilities, arrange repainting and repairs, assign caretakers and managers to each property, and remit to landlords with statements.
  • Landlords, developers and agents who manage their own portfolios or advertise property for rent or sale.

A public marketplace, added in Release 3, lets house hunters and businesses browse homes, offices, shops, warehouses and land for rent or sale. It draws on the vacancies of properties already managed on the platform, so listings are verified at the source and a successful enquiry flows straight into a lease or a sale contract.

The minimum viable product, Release 1, is deliberately tight. It builds the multi-tenant core and the full estate management workflow, and launches Shaba Village as the first tenant within 14 weeks. Rental and portfolio management for property managers follows in Release 2, and the marketplace in Release 3, on the same data model, so nothing built for Shaba is rebuilt later.

What Shaba Village gets at launch

  • One balance per unit and fund, visible to each owner on their phone, with statements on demand.
  • Paybill payments matched to the right unit and receipted within a minute.
  • Monthly bills, including metered water with photo evidence, issued for every unit in one run.
  • Instalment schedules, reminders and purchase statements from reservation to handover and title.
  • Sale agreements, handover forms, clearance certificates and notices generated from templates and stored against each unit.
  • Service provider contracts, schedules, guard posts and patrols, with vendor invoices adjusted for missed service.
  • Visitor passes and gate logs that collect only what the law allows.
  • Estate staff and casual workers paid through the ERP payroll; sales and estate money kept in separate ledgers.

Introduction

Purpose

This document specifies the requirements and design of the Codevertex Property platform across its releases, the delivery plan for Release 1, and the commercial terms for Shaba Village as launch tenant. It is the basis for development, testing and acceptance, and replaces the earlier property management requirements document.

Product vision

Kenyan property businesses run on spreadsheets, paybill statements, WhatsApp groups and paper files. Rent and service charge are reconciled by hand, deposits disappear into general accounts, repairs are chased by phone, and house hunters lose money to fake listings. Codevertex Property gives each business one system of record for its properties, people, agreements and money, and gives the public one place to find property that is real, available and verified.

Launch tenant: Shaba Village

Shaba Village is a residential estate in Syokimau, off Mombasa Road, in the Mavoko area of Machakos County. It comprises homes and apartments, including two and three bedroom units, sold outright or on deposit and instalments. Estate management supplies water from the Mavoko Water and Sewerage Company connection and two boreholes through an underground reservoir and unit tanks, and runs perimeter security, 24-hour guarding, landscaping, cleaning, garbage collection, sewer and parking, all billed to owners every month. Shaba's needs define Release 1; its configuration is described in Estate management: Shaba Village configuration.

Positioning

Local tools collect rent but do not run an estate, sell units or manage providers; global tools are priced in dollars and do not speak M-Pesa, eTIMS or Kenyan tax. Codevertex Property combines both, and it is the only product in the segment that sits on a full accounting, payroll and messaging stack already in production.

Overall description

Product perspective

Each customer is a tenant of the Codevertex platform. Three new components, property-ui, property-api and, in Release 3, marketplace-ui, provide the property product. Sign-in, invoicing, M-Pesa, the ledger, approvals, payroll, messaging, plans, leads and maps come from existing platform services, described in Solution architecture. The property service holds references to their records and never keeps a second copy.

Operating environment

  • All users: any modern mobile or desktop browser; installable as a progressive web application
  • Gate: an Android tablet at each gate in kiosk mode, with offline operation during network loss
  • Hosting: Codevertex Kubernetes platform with PostgreSQL, Redis, NATS JetStream and object storage, behind Cloudflare
  • Payments: Safaricom M-Pesa through each tenant's own paybills (Daraja API) and bank transfers recorded against unit accounts
  • Messaging: Africa's Talking (SMS), Meta WhatsApp Cloud API, Codevertex mail server (email)

Constraints

  • Money is collected only into each tenant's own paybills and bank accounts. Codevertex never receives tenant or customer funds.
  • The property service does not move money. Invoices, payments and payouts are executed by the treasury service.
  • Utility supply is never cut off automatically, and nobody is locked out, for arrears. Kenyan courts and tribunals have held self-help disconnection unlawful.
  • Electricity is not resold; units use Kenya Power prepaid meters.
  • Conveyancing, lease registration and legal notices are performed by advocates; the platform generates drafts, stores signed documents and records progress.

Assumptions and dependencies for the Shaba launch

  • Shaba Village supplies the unit register, owner list, sale agreements, templates of its existing forms and opening balances by week 4, in the import template provided.
  • Shaba Village holds, or opens, separate paybills and bank accounts for sale collections and for the estate fund, and grants Daraja API access by week 3.
  • Shaba Village confirms the charge schedule, water tariff, penalty rules and allocation order from its by-laws and sale agreements by week 5.
  • Service provider contracts and licences are available for loading by week 8.
  • Shaba Village appoints a product owner able to decide within 2 working days and supply documents within 3.
  • Shaba Village registers with the ODPC as a data controller and engages an advocate to approve the legal pack and document templates.
  • Shaba Village provides one Android tablet per gate, with a data connection.
  • Messaging, payment provider charges and the domain name are client-borne costs, as set out in Commercial terms.
