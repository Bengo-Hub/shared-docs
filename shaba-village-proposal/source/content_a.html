Executive summary

Codevertex Africa Limited proposes to design, build and launch the Shaba Village Estate Platform. The platform runs the estate's two money streams and its daily operations in one place. The first stream is the sale of homes and apartments, paid outright or by deposit and instalments. The second is the monthly billing of water, service charge, garbage collection, security and other estate charges to every owner.

Owners see their purchase progress, bills, meter readings and receipts on their phone and pay by M-Pesa against their unit number. Payments are matched and receipted automatically. The management team works from one console covering units, billing, arrears, sales, maintenance, service providers, gate security and reports. Security, cleaning and garbage agencies work through the same platform, and their invoices are checked against the service they actually delivered before payment.

The platform is built on Codevertex's production services for sign-in, accounting, M-Pesa collections, payroll and messaging. Only the estate-specific service and its screens are new, which keeps the build price and the delivery time low.

Key outcomes

  • Every unit carries one balance per fund, visible to the owner at any time, with statements on demand.
  • Paybill payments matched to the right unit and receipted within a minute, with no manual reconciliation for correctly referenced payments.
  • Monthly bills, including metered water, issued for all units in one run on the 1st, with reading photos as evidence.
  • Instalment schedules, reminders and purchase statements for every buyer, from reservation to handover and title.
  • Service provider contracts, schedules, guard posts and patrols tracked, and vendor invoices adjusted for missed service before payment.
  • Visitor passes and gate logs that protect residents while collecting only the data the law allows.
  • Estate staff, including casual workers, paid through the ERP payroll with statutory deductions.
  • Sales money and estate service money kept in separate accounts and ledgers, ready for handover to the owners' management corporation.

Introduction

Purpose

This document specifies the requirements, design, delivery plan and commercial terms for the Shaba Village Estate Platform. It is the basis for development, testing and acceptance, and replaces the earlier generic property management requirements document.

About Shaba Village

Shaba Village is a residential estate in Syokimau, off Mombasa Road, in the Mavoko area of Machakos County. It comprises homes and apartments, including two and three bedroom units. Units are sold outright or on a deposit and instalment basis. Estate management supplies water from the Mavoko Water and Sewerage Company connection and two boreholes, stored in an underground reservoir and unit tanks, and runs shared services: perimeter security with electric fencing, 24-hour guarding, landscaping, cleaning of common areas, garbage collection, sewer and parking. These services are billed to owners every month.

Shaba Village operates as a tenant of the Codevertex platform. Two new components, estates-ui and estates-api, provide the estate product. Operating environment

  • Owners, occupants and vendors: any modern mobile or desktop browser; installable as a progressive web application
  • Gate: an Android tablet at each gate in kiosk mode, with offline operation during network loss
  • Hosting: Codevertex Kubernetes platform with PostgreSQL, Redis, NATS JetStream and object storage, behind Cloudflare
  • Payments: Safaricom M-Pesa through Shaba Village's own paybills (Daraja API); bank transfers recorded against unit accounts
  • Messaging: Africa's Talking (SMS), Meta WhatsApp Cloud API, Codevertex mail server (email)

Constraints

  • Money is collected only into Shaba Village's own paybills and bank accounts. Codevertex never receives estate or buyer funds.
  • The estate service does not move money. Invoices, payments and payouts are executed by the treasury service.
  • Utility supply is never cut off automatically for arrears. Kenyan courts and tribunals have held that disconnection without due process is unlawful.
  • Electricity is supplied by Kenya Power prepaid meters per unit and is not resold by the estate.
  • Conveyancing, title registration and legal notices are performed by advocates; the platform records their progress.

Who receives the bill

The owner is liable for all estate charges by default. Where an owner lets the unit, the owner may assign water, garbage and parking to the tenant, who then receives those bills and pays with the same unit account number. Service charge, sinking fund and special levies remain with the owner. If an assigned tenant leaves with an unpaid balance, the balance reverts to the owner after 30 days, as the owner terms state.

Fund segregation

Sale proceeds belong to the developer. Estate charges are collected to run the estate on behalf of all owners. Mixing the two is the most common cause of service charge disputes in Kenyan estates. The platform keeps them apart by design: separate paybills and bank accounts, separate cost centres and ledger accounts in treasury, and separate statements. A buyer who is also an owner sees both balances, but a payment to one account can never settle the other.

Transition to the management corporation

Under the Sectional Properties Act, 2020, common property in a sectionally registered estate is owned and managed by a management corporation of the unit owners. The estate fund books, unit accounts, sinking fund, vendor contracts and records are kept so that they can be handed over complete when the corporation is formed. Shaba Village can then continue as managing agent for the corporation on the same platform, or hand over administrative control entirely.
