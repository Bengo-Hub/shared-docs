Who receives the bill

The owner is liable for all estate charges by default. Where an owner lets the unit, the owner may assign water, garbage and parking to the tenant, who then receives those bills and pays with the same unit account number. Service charge, sinking fund and special levies remain with the owner. If an assigned tenant leaves with an unpaid balance, the balance reverts to the owner after 30 days, as the owner terms state.

Fund segregation

Sale proceeds belong to the developer. Estate charges are collected to run the estate on behalf of all owners. Mixing the two is the most common cause of service charge disputes in Kenyan estates. The platform keeps them apart by design: separate paybills and bank accounts, separate cost centres and ledger accounts in treasury, and separate statements. A buyer who is also an owner sees both balances, but a payment to one account can never settle the other.

Transition to the management corporation

Under the Sectional Properties Act, 2020, common property in a sectionally registered estate is owned and managed by a management corporation of the unit owners. The estate fund books, unit accounts, sinking fund, vendor contracts and records are kept so that they can be handed over complete when the corporation is formed. Shaba Village can then continue as managing agent for the corporation on the same platform, or hand over administrative control entirely.

Billing, utilities and collections

Monthly cycle

Dates are configurable. The billing run asks treasury to create one invoice per unit and fund with the month's line items. Each invoice carries the unit code as its payment reference and a link to pay. Owners receive a short message with the amount, due date and paybill details, and the full bill in the portal.

Meter readings

  1. The caretaker opens the reading round on a phone. Units are listed in walking order by block.
  2. Each reading is typed and photographed. The photo is stored with the reading and shown on the bill.
  3. The platform flags readings lower than the previous one, zero consumption for an occupied unit, and consumption above three times the unit's 3-month average.
  4. Flagged readings are re-checked. A meter that cannot be read is billed at the 3-month average, marked estimated, and corrected on the next actual reading.
  5. A meter replacement records the closing reading of the old meter and the opening reading of the new one, so no consumption is lost or billed twice.

Water balance

Bulk readings from the Mavoko connection and each borehole meter are entered monthly. The report compares total water supplied with water billed to units and estimated common area use. The difference is unaccounted water, shown as a percentage and trended by month. Above the set threshold, for example 15%, the estate manager is alerted to check for leaks.

Payment matching and allocation

  • Each M-Pesa confirmation is stored once under its receipt number, so a repeated callback never creates a second payment.
  • The account number is normalised (case, spaces, dashes) and matched to the unit. Close misses, such as b 07 or B7, are matched; anything else goes to the suspense queue.
  • Allocation follows the configured order: oldest invoice first by default, or charge type priority if the by-laws require it. Late charges are settled last.
  • Daily, treasury compares the paybill statement with recorded payments. Differences are listed for finance.

Not automated: disconnection of water or any other supply, locking of gates or denial of entry to a resident for arrears. These require due process and are excluded from the platform by design.

Budget and transparency

The annual service charge budget is entered in treasury by cost line: security, cleaning, garbage, landscaping, water production, common power, repairs, management, insurance and the sinking fund. Each vendor bill and payroll cost is posted to the matching line. Owners can see a monthly summary of budget against actual, and the income and expenditure statement is produced for the AGM directly from the ledger.

Property staff

Each property has its team: a property manager, one or more caretakers, and finance and letting officers as needed, all assigned in the console. Staff see the properties assigned to them; a caretaker's phone shows only their property's readings, requests, inspections and notices. Firm-employed staff are paid through the ERP; landlord-paid caretakers can be recorded as a property expense instead.

Tenant onboarding

  1. A vacant unit is advertised, from the console or (Release 3) on the marketplace, or an applicant is entered by the letting officer.
  2. The applicant applies on their phone: identity, contacts, employer or business details, income evidence, references and next of kin, with consent to the checks described in the privacy notice.
  3. The letting officer reviews; where the mandate requires, the landlord approves in the portal.
  4. The lease is generated from the tenant's template with the agreed terms, sent for acceptance by OTP or signature, and stored once executed.
  5. The first invoice for deposit, first rent and any move-in fee is issued; on payment the lease activates.
  6. The move-in inspection records each room's condition with photos, meter readings and keys handed over, signed by both sides. The lessee gets portal access, gate passes and the house rules.

Rent and charges

  • Rent and recurring charges invoice on the lease schedule (monthly, quarterly or as agreed) with utilities from meter readings and service charge from the catalogue.
  • Each lease has its own paybill account reference, so rent payments match automatically, as for owners in Release 1.
  • Escalations apply on the lease's review dates (fixed percentage or agreed amount), with a notice letter generated in advance as the lease requires.
  • Commercial leases carry VAT on rent where the landlord is registered, service charge recovery, rent-free periods and lessee withholding tax on rent, with the withholding certificate captured against the payment.

Deposits

Deposits are liabilities, not income. Each deposit is held in a deposit ledger against the lease, shown on the lessee's and landlord's statements, and never counted in rent remitted. On move-out, deductions for unpaid rent, utilities and damage beyond fair wear and tear are itemised with the inspection photos, approved, and the balance refunded to the lessee within the period in the lease, typically 30 days.

Turnover: repaint and repairs

When a unit is coming vacant, a turnover project opens with tasks from the move-out inspection: repainting, plumbing and electrical repairs, deep cleaning, pest control and replacement items. Each task is a work order with a quote, cost and photos. Costs above the mandate limit go to the landlord for approval. The project sets a ready date, which drives the listing's availability date.

Templates

  • Codevertex seeds starter templates for each tenant type. Each tenant adapts them, or uploads its advocate's own wording, before use. Templates are versioned and must be approved by the tenant administrator before they can be issued.
  • Merge fields pull from the record: parties, unit, property, prices, schedules, dates, meter readings, balances. Conditional clauses switch on configuration, for example a guarantor clause only when a guarantor exists.
  • Generated documents are PDFs in the tenant's branding with a reference number and a verification code, so a copy can be checked against the original.

Each document moves through draft, issued, partly signed, executed, and expired or superseded. Key dates in documents (lease end, review dates, notice deadlines, licence expiry) become reminders. Leases over two years must be registered under the Land Registration Act, and leases attract stamp duty under the Stamp Duty Act; the platform flags both on generation and records the registration and stamp duty references when the advocate returns them.

Document storage and access

  • Stored in object storage under the tenant, linked to the unit, parties and contract, never public; opened through signed links that expire.
  • Access by role and relationship: a lessee sees their own lease and inspection reports; a landlord sees the documents of their portfolio; identity documents are visible only to the sales or letting role.
  • Every view and download of a contract or identity document is logged.
  • Retention follows the schedule in Legal and regulatory compliance; documents under a legal hold are not purged.

Security agency operations

  1. Posts are defined with the number of guards per shift, for example main gate two by day and two by night, and a night patrol.
  2. The agency supervisor uploads the weekly roster through the vendor portal. Only guards registered for the estate can be rostered.
  3. Guards sign on and off at the gate tablet with their badge and PIN. The platform compares sign-ons with the roster and contract and reports unmanned post time.
  4. Patrol rounds are scheduled; guards scan QR tags at each checkpoint. Missed checkpoints are reported in the morning summary.
  5. Incidents and handover notes are written in the digital occurrence book, visible to the estate manager in real time.
  6. At month end, the coverage report is attached to the agency's invoice with any service credit due under the contract.

Agency personnel

The agency is the employer of its guards and cleaners and remains responsible for their vetting, pay and statutory obligations. Shaba Village records only what it needs to control access: name, agency, role, phone number and badge. A photo is stored only if the agency provides it for identification at the gate. When a person leaves the estate deployment, the badge is deactivated the same day.

Vendor invoices and payment

  1. The vendor submits the monthly invoice in the vendor portal with its eTIMS invoice number and document.
  2. The platform places the invoice beside the contract fee, completed and missed visits, post coverage and resulting service credit.
  3. The estate manager confirms the service; treasury records the vendor bill against the budget line.
  4. Approval follows the payout policy: below the limit it is approved by finance, above it by a director with OTP, bound to amount and payee.
  5. Treasury pays from the estate account by bank transfer or M-Pesa, deducts withholding tax where it applies, and sends the remittance advice.

Estate staff

Shaba Village's own regular and casual staff, such as the caretaker, plumber, gardeners and day labourers, are managed in the ERP. Regular staff are paid through monthly payroll with PAYE, NSSF, SHIF and the affordable housing levy. Casual workers are paid per day or per task from the casual payments module, by M-Pesa, with the payment recorded against the work order's cost line. Payroll and casual payments post to the estate fund ledger in treasury. property-api only stores the ERP employee ID on work orders and service checks.

Data collected at the gate

The gate collects the minimum needed for security: the visitor's name, phone number, host unit, time in and out, and vehicle plate if driving. Identity cards are not photographed, copied or retained. Where the by-laws require an identity check, the guard views the card and records only that it was seen. A privacy notice is displayed at each gate and linked in every pass message. Gate events are kept for 90 days and incident records for the period set in the retention schedule.

Offline operation

Each gate tablet keeps a cache of passes valid for the next 24 hours and of active badges. During a network outage, passes are verified from the cache and new entries are queued on the device, encrypted, and sent when the connection returns. A tablet offline for more than 15 minutes raises an alert to the estate manager.

Incidents and emergencies

  • Incidents are categorised (intrusion, theft, fire, medical, dispute, damage, other) and timed, with photos.
  • Serious incidents notify the estate manager and the agency supervisor at once by WhatsApp and SMS.
  • Emergency broadcasts reach all residents in a block or the whole estate in one action.
