01 Verdict and Recommendation
Codevertex Africa has spent the past two years building and now runs a set of business
software used by companies across Kenya today: point-of-sale and inventory management,
online ordering, delivery logistics, accounting and payments, subscription billing, and a
system that sends email, SMS, and WhatsApp messages on a client's behalf. All of it sits
behind one shared login, so a business signs up once and turns on whichever pieces it
needs. Codevertex also built and runs a CRM and marketing-automation product called
MarketFlow, originally for a direct-selling client and now being adapted for other kinds of
businesses.
None of that was built with a creator marketplace in mind, and none of it does what AELIA
Creator Commerce needs to do on its own. But a large part of what any new marketplace
needs on the plumbing side, meaning how a person logs in, how money moves safely
between two parties, how a subscription gets billed, how a message gets sent, already exists
inside Codevertex and is already running in production for other businesses. That is the
opportunity this report tests: whether AELIA can get built faster by connecting to that existing
plumbing rather than building all of it again from the beginning.

The recommendation
AELIA needs its own new software, built specifically around brands and creators
working together, because that two-sided relationship does not fit the shape of anything
Codevertex already runs. Its data models are all built for a single business managing its
own customers, MarketFlow included, and forcing a brand-to-creator relationship into
that shape would mean fighting the underlying database design at every turn.
What AELIA does not need is to build its supporting infrastructure from scratch. The
plan in this report connects it, from day one, to five things Codevertex already operates
in production: the login and account system, the payments and payout engine, the
subscription billing system, the messaging system for email, SMS, and WhatsApp, and
the AI assistant already running inside MarketFlow.
That combination should get AELIA to a real pilot fast, using infrastructure that is
already proven rather than untested.

02 What We Reviewed, and Corrections to Make
Both documents are unusually disciplined for a concept stage. The same prove-the-workflow-first principle repeats throughout, and the developer brief is careful to flag its own technical
choices as recommendations rather than decisions. Six things still need correcting, informed by how Codevertex's own systems work today and current Kenyan market conditions.
ISSUE WHAT THE DOCUMENTS SAY WHAT NEEDS TO CHANGE
Mobile scope contradicts the
timeline
BLOCKS THE 3-MONTH MVP
The Concept paper lists native apps under what not to build first. The
Developer Brief then overrides that and requires Android and iOS store apps
as a launch requirement.
Follow the Concept paper's original sequencing. A mobile-first responsive web app
should be the MVP, installable to a phone's home screen the same way Codevertex
already does elsewhere in its platform. Native apps become a later-phase item once
the pilot has validated demand.
Recommended stack forfeits
everything reusable
COSTS 4–6 WEEKS
The brief recommends a NestJS and Node.js backend, while noting the
engineer may propose an equivalent stack.
Use Codevertex's own standard: Go for Aelia's backend, Next.js for Aelia's web app,
the same stack as every other Codevertex product. This is what makes the reuse case
on the following pages actually usable, since Codevertex's shared toolkit only drops in
cleanly on a Go backend.
A homegrown escrow is not a real
option
LEGAL
Both documents correctly flag that AELIA should not assume it can operate an
internal escrow service, but stop short of naming what is required.
Holding client funds in Kenya requires a Payment Service Provider licence from the
Central Bank of Kenya, a process that takes months. The good news: the Payments
Service already pays out to third parties over licensed M-Pesa and Paystack rails today.
Adding "creator" as a payee type is the right move, not a new PSP relationship.
The commission math skips a live
tax rule
CHANGES THE NUMBERS
The illustrative example shows a KES 20,000 fee, an 8% commission, and an
18,400 payout, with tax only a general mention.
Kenya's Finance Act 2023 digital-content withholding tax is now enforced. Meta,
YouTube, and TikTok already deduct 5% at source. The withholding itself is mandatory
on Aelia as the payer, not optional; what is creator-initiated is the KRA PIN registration
that has to happen first so Aelia can remit it correctly. This belongs in the MVP, not a
later phase.

04 How Aelia Plugs Into the Codevertex Ecosystem
Aelia gets its own new system rather than living inside MarketFlow. What matters here is how it connects to what Codevertex already runs, service by service, and the pattern it follows to do
that without creating two copies of the same data anywhere on the platform.
How Aelia connects without duplicating data
When one Codevertex product needs another's data, it never copies that data into its own database. It stores a simple reference and listens for updates. Several of Codevertex's other
business products already do this with MarketFlow today, keeping a pointer to the relevant MarketFlow customer record rather than duplicating it, and pulling the live details only when
they actually need them. That is the integration shape Aelia follows too: its own database, a thin reference back into MarketFlow only if a brand's Aelia activity ever needs to enrich its
MarketFlow record, and live updates either way. The accounting system's own quotation feature shows the same pattern with a small addition worth copying directly: it stores a
reference to the relevant MarketFlow customer, plus a cached copy of their name and email, so a quote can display a customer's name without a live lookup every time it is opened.
Aelia's own campaign records do the same with a brand or creator reference and a cached display name, and nothing more.
Aelia Brand House has three arms. This report builds one of them.
Aelia Holdings runs three connected businesses: AELIA Marketing, the agency doing standard marketing-agency work; AELIA Creative & Digital, which produces content and trains
creators through its Academy; and the Creator Commerce Platform this report is about. That means two websites, not one. A general Aelia Brand House site introduces all three, a low-
complexity parallel build that does not compete with the three-month marketplace build for engineering time. The Creator Commerce Platform gets its own dedicated site and domain,
the "Aelia's web app" this report designs from here on. Wherever this report says Aelia's own team or channels for producing or distributing content, that means AELIA Creative & Digital
and AELIA Marketing specifically, not this platform's own engineering team.

05 How One Campaign Actually Moves Through the Platform
This is the exact sequence the platform needs to support, spelled out step by step because the diagram on the previous page compresses it.
STEP WHAT HAPPENS
1 A brand posts a campaign brief: objective, audience, platform, deliverables, and budget. No money moves yet.
2 AELIA Match generates options against that brief: creators whose Passport fits the niche, AELIA Creative & Digital's own production when the budget matches its rates, and, where it fits, the AI-
generated lane, run by AELIA Creative & Digital rather than the brand. The brand sees whichever apply, side by side.
3 The brand picks one option, or shortlists a few. This is a preference, not a booking yet.
4 Someone at Aelia, not just an algorithm, reviews the brand's pick against the brief and confirms it is genuinely the best fit, or suggests an adjustment. This is the point where a human catches a bad
match before a brand's money or a creator's time is committed.
5 Aelia contacts the chosen creator directly and arranges an introduction with the brand, a real conversation, not an automated notification the creator might miss. Steps 5 and 6 assume an external
creator; the in-house and AI lanes skip straight to production once funded, since AELIA Creative & Digital is already the party doing the work, and the AI lane can also publish through AELIA Marketing's
own channels as an add-on.
6 Brand and creator agree the specific terms, deliverables, deadline, fee, usage rights, and the payout schedule below, and the campaign agreement is signed. In practice, the brand accepts Aelia's
campaign terms and the creator accepts Aelia's creator terms for this brief, not necessarily one document both sign together.
7 Only now does the brand fund the campaign, paid to Aelia through the licensed payment rail, never held as a private balance. A brand paying by direct bank transfer instead, common for corporates,
uploads proof of payment for Aelia's finance team to verify before the campaign moves to funded, extending a manual-payment path the Payments Service already supports.
8 Aelia's commission is set aside immediately. What happens next to the creator's share depends on the payout schedule agreed in step 6.
9 The creator produces and submits the content. The brand reviews it, asks for permitted revisions if needed, and approves it.
10 Whatever portion of the fee was not already released in step 8 is paid out now, with the 5% KRA withholding applied wherever the money actually reaches the creator. Both sides can rate each other,
and the brand can rebook the same creator directly next time.
Payout timing is a term of the agreement, not a fixed platform rule
SCHEDULE HOW IT WORKS WHEN IT FITS

07 Payments, Commission, and Compliance
Extend the existing payout rail
Holding client money in Kenya requires a PSP licence from the Central Bank of Kenya, a months-
long process. The Payments Service already disburses over licensed M-Pesa and Paystack rails
today; adding "creator" as a payee type is the right move. The one legal sign-off needed: use
Paystack's split-payment feature for the brand-funding leg so commission and payout route at the
processor level in real time, rather than sitting in a Codevertex-held balance.
5% KRA withholding is mandatory, not optional
Kenya's Finance Act 2023 digital-content withholding tax is now actively enforced. Meta,
YouTube, and TikTok already deduct 5% at source. The withholding is mandatory on Aelia as
payer; the KRA PIN registration collected during onboarding is the creator-initiated step that
makes remitting it correctly possible. Required for the MVP, not added later.
Data Protection Act, 2019
Both source documents call for privacy by design without naming the law. AELIA becomes a
registered data controller the moment it stores creator and brand data at scale. ODPC registration
is a Phase 0 legal task, with a real consent log and data-subject-request flow from day one.
Sponsored-content disclosure
MarketFlow's existing compliance filter is specific to its original client and does not carry over.
AELIA needs its own lightweight content-safety module: a disclosure tag on published content
and a brand-safety report path in the admin console.

07 What Creators and Brands Actually Pay
Neither source document commits to a number here on purpose: the concept paper lists pricing itself as a question the manual-first pilot is meant to answer by asking real brands, not
guessing. This report keeps that discipline, but a technical partnership still needs a starting point to build against, so this is a structure to validate, not a final price list.
Creators pay nothing to join
Signing up, building a Creator Passport, and receiving campaign invitations all stay free. The only
charge a creator ever sees is the platform commission, taken out of a payout only when they
actually earn, matching the concept paper's own principle of keeping basic participation
accessible.

09 Legal Documents and Contracts
A creator marketplace sits between more parties, and moves more money between them, than almost anything else Codevertex has built. This section maps what needs to exist, and
resolves one structural question the source documents leave open.
The decision this report makes: Aelia stands in the middle of every deal, not beside it
Brand pays Aelia, and Aelia, after its cut, pays the creator. That means Aelia is not simply introducing two parties who then strike their own deal. It is the counterparty to both sides at
once: selling a campaign service to the brand, and buying a creator's content or influence for it. In practice, Aelia signs its own agreement with the brand for a campaign, and its own
agreement with the creator for the same campaign, tied together by one shared reference. The brand and creator work together directly once introduced, but sign nothing with each
other, only with Aelia. The one right a brand needs from a creator, permission to use content in its own advertising, is granted to Aelia under the creator's agreement and passed on to
the brand under the brand's agreement. Confirm this shape with a Kenyan commercial lawyer before the first real contract goes out, since it also decides how tax applies to Aelia's own
commission.

11 Partnership and Equity Terms
Framed as a technology partner and CTO joining at idea or pre-seed stage, not a contractor billing for a scope of work, the ask below sits inside the normal market range for what is being
contributed.
The real alternative isn't a cheaper CTO. It's hiring a contractor every time something needs building.
Without a technical co-founder, Aelia's other path is hiring this out: an agency for the initial build, then a fresh engagement every time something changes afterward, a new AI vendor
integration when HeyGen or Creatify change pricing or API, the day-to-day work of running the AI-generated ad pipeline and keeping it priced correctly, a payment rail for the move into
Uganda or Tanzania, a security patch, a feature a competitor just shipped. Each restarts the same three costs. Money: at current Kenyan market rates, building this scope from zero,
without Codevertex's existing login, payments, billing, and notification infrastructure, is a reasonable planning estimate of KES 7 million to 13 million, roughly $50,000 to $90,000, for the
six-month build alone, before an ongoing maintenance retainer typically running 15 to 20 percent of that a year. Security: a contractor builds a system handling money and personal
data once and moves to the next client; Codevertex's patterns are already carrying that responsibility in production for other paying businesses today. Confidentiality: every external
engagement hands a third party the commission structure and creator and brand data, with no ongoing stake in protecting any of it once the invoice is paid. An equity-aligned CTO is
paid, in the same currency as the founder, to protect exactly that.
The technical weight behind 30 percent
Not a brochure site. A real per-transaction commission ledger with no precedent anywhere in Codevertex's systems. A multi-party payment flow holding funds through Paystack's split-
payment mechanism, automating Kenya's 5% withholding tax, and reconciling manual bank transfers against uploaded proof. An AI content pipeline operating third-party vendors on
Aelia's behalf, priced correctly against real cost. A compliance surface spanning the Data Protection Act, KRA withholding, and content disclosure that has to be right from day one.
None of it ends at launch: matching quality, vendor relationships, and security posture all need continuous technical ownership, exactly what the CTO role and equity below are
structured to cover.
